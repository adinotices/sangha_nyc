The Sit

One evening a week, we put the phones down and sit together.

If you've never meditated a day in your life, this is a good place to start. Nothing to prepare, nothing to prove.

New here? Just show up — or send a note first if you'd rather know a face before you walk in. You don't need to sign up, pay, or know anything about Buddhism to come sit with us.

We keep it simple and unpretentious: a little quiet, a short teaching, a real sit, and time to actually talk to each other afterward. The point isn't to perform serenity — it's to practice honestly, in good company, and head back out into the city a little more awake.

✦ The shape of an evening
  1. 01

    Arrive & settle ~5 min

    Come in, find a cushion or a chair, and drop into a few minutes of silence to let the day fall away.

  2. 02

    Welcome & check-in ~10 min

    Quick introductions and a chance to name how you're actually arriving — no need to have it together.

  3. 03

    Guided meditation & instruction ~15 min

    A short guided practice with instruction, so newer and seasoned sitters alike have something to work with.

  4. 04

    Silent sit ~25 min

    The heart of the evening. We meditate together in silence — the thing that's genuinely hard to do alone.

  5. 05

    Dharma & reflection ~15 min

    A brief talk or a passage on practice, ethics, and everyday life — then space to sit with what it stirs up.

  6. 06

    Small-group sharing ~15 min

    We break into pairs or small groups to talk honestly about practice, the reading, or whatever's alive for you.

  7. 07

    Closing mettā ~5 min

    We end with a loving-kindness meditation — for ourselves, for each other, for the city outside.

  8. 08

    Hang out optional

    Tea, snacks, and unhurried conversation. For a lot of people, this is where the sangha actually happens.

Good to Know

Frequently asked

Do I need any prior experience?

No. Most people who come have little or none — plenty are sitting down to meditate for the very first time. Every sit includes simple instruction, and there's nothing to prepare or read beforehand. Just come as you are.

What does it cost?

Nothing — our sits are free. We do gratefully accept donations (in the Buddhist tradition of dāna, or generosity), which help cover the space and keep everything open to everyone. But a donation is never expected, and never a condition of sitting with us.

What age do I have to be?

We decided not to set a specific age range, because we didn't want to exclude anyone outside of that range who feels like a young adult. And even if you don't identify as a young adult — you're welcome here too!
